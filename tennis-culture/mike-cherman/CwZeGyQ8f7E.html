  1. 00:00:16

    The office begins with shipping and design

    Mike opens a tour of the still-new Six Ounce Studio space. MARKET’s receiving and shipping area remains visibly DIY, while the nearby design office is already operating inside the same hot warehouse.

  2. 00:00:44

    Every order is handled in-house

    The company ships orders on the day they are placed. Mike introduces the illustrators, designers, agency staff, and warehouse team as one operation rather than separating the creative image from fulfillment.

  3. 00:01:12

    Young staff learn the whole product path

    Photography, video, marketing, social media, warehousing, and design happen around the same group. Mike calls them a cast of younger outsiders who can get their hands dirty and learn how an idea becomes an actual shipped object.

  4. 00:01:41

    Mike wants the team to repeat his learning trajectory

    He contrasts the current office with his start alone in a Brooklyn print studio. Employees should understand product creation, storage, and shipping, and they should be allowed to fail at manageable scale so the lesson becomes their own.

  5. 00:02:10

    A shoe prototype can begin with a recent conversation

    Mike shows a Force shoe experiment that grew from an idea during a photo shoot only a week or two earlier. The point is not the finished release; it is the speed between noticing an idea and testing it.

  6. 00:02:44

    The prototype room keeps capabilities close

    The room contains pretreatment and digital-printing equipment, a heat press, embroidery, straight-stitch and five-stitch machines, and a serger. The team can make a first version immediately before sending a developed idea to a factory.

  7. 00:03:13

    Applications and ownership are taught together

    Rhinestones, digital transfers, and other techniques are also handled internally. Mike connects this access to his own experience of being exploited when he was young and says he wants staff to gain enough knowledge to make independent products.

  8. 00:03:41

    Growth came one person at a time

    What had been one person designing, producing, and shipping became a team of roughly fifteen. Mike describes the change as exciting and slightly unbelievable rather than as a smooth, preplanned expansion.

  9. 00:04:10

    The durable motive is still making shirts

    Mike closes by separating business scale from the underlying practice. Whether the work becomes a brand or a client service, he wants to keep making shirts, having fun, and building an operation that lets other people do the same.
