Chinatown Market's Mike Cherman and Dan Altmann: An Interview at Sole DXB

Sneakers Middle East · 2019-12-11 · 00:07:10 · interview

Summary

  • What this is: A short Sole DXB interview about positivity, MARKET’s expansion from clothing into content, and the controlled chaos created when a team contributes different perspectives.
  • Context: A 00:07:10 interview published by Sneakers Middle East on 2019-12-11.
  • Positivity is the organizing idea. The smiley matters because the brand wants to offer an accessible counterweight to negative cultural energy.
  • Product and content are one system. Mike describes company growth as marrying what it makes with how those objects circulate as stories.
  • Team participation creates controlled chaos. Unexpected posts and ideas can work because multiple people are allowed to shape the brand voice.

Transcript summary

  1. The format is an informal conversation

    Mike Cherman and Dan Altmann introduce themselves from Chinatown Market and agree that the exchange should feel like a direct chat rather than a formal founder interview.

  2. The smiley stands for encouragement

    Mike says the recurring smiley is less important as a specific image than as a signal of positivity. The brand wants younger people without obvious support to believe that making something themselves is attainable.

  3. Accessibility is part of the message

    The stated goal is to change the idea that creative work belongs to a remote class of people. The hosts briefly move from the smiley into how Mike and Dan began working together.

  4. A project and proximity created the partnership

    They first crossed paths through entertainment and Disney-related work, then became neighbors in Los Angeles. Their conversations expanded from clothing toward a larger creative and media business.

  5. The relationship formed through repeated help

    Mike situates the partnership after the loss of his previous company. Dan initially tried to connect him with other people, but Mike repeatedly returned to Dan for advice until the working relationship emerged organically.

  6. Product and content are treated as one system

    They reject a clean boundary between media and merchandise: a product can carry a story, while content can create the context in which the product matters. Their challenge is to develop both without imitating an established conglomerate.

  7. Education can lengthen the customer relationship

    Free viewing, liking, and commenting do not automatically create a durable business. The team wants people to learn from the company and its staff, participate in education, and remain connected even during periods when they do not want a particular product.

  8. The ambition extends beyond being today’s brand

    Mike and Dan describe a company that teaches a next generation and grows through media, education, and product. That broader relationship is intended to outlast a short fashion cycle.

  9. Instagram is approached as a participant would use it

    Even platform specialists ask what Chinatown Market is doing differently. The answer is to avoid overprotecting the feed and to behave more like the people using the platform than like a conventional advertiser.

  10. Many staff perspectives create the voice

    The social account became more varied when multiple people in the office could contribute memes, images, and observations. Different contributors let the brand speak to communities one centralized marketing voice would miss.

  11. The content system allows both chaos and production

    Some posts are planned and some begin as an employee’s spontaneous joke. The team calls this controlled chaos, while also producing higher-budget programs with media partners when the idea needs a formal shoot.

  12. High and low formats belong together

    Memes, products, and produced shows all express the same basic habit: respond to what is happening, contribute a point of view, and encourage the audience to create rather than merely consume.

  13. Downtime is deliberate disconnection

    Mike says constant stimulation at the office leaves him needing six to eight hours of low-demand time at home. Rest is described as turning the brain off after being socially and creatively available all day.

  14. Travel is exposure to another community

    The conversation closes with gratitude for meeting people in Dubai and seeing a different scene. The remaining exchange is casual travel talk, reinforcing that the visit is partly about being present with the local community rather than extracting a campaign image.
