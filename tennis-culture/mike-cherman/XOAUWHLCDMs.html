  1. 00:00:00

    Failure is presented as the learning mechanism

    Mike opens by saying mistakes define him because he learns only after falling hard. He identifies as a graphic designer who makes clothing, logos, brands, and other tangible expressions.

  2. 00:00:29

    The film names the contradiction around Chinatown Market

    MAEKAN introduces Mike as a white founder whose latest brand uses Chinatown in its name, then notes criticism from Asian audiences. The documentary postpones judgment long enough to establish the career history behind the conflict.

  3. 00:00:57

    ICNY’s loss precedes the Los Angeles restart

    By twenty-five, Mike had already experienced sharp business highs and an ugly investor split. Moving to Los Angeles created distance from ICNY and enough clarity to question how much he actually cared about conventional fashion.

  4. 00:01:53

    The central topic is an in-between career

    The interview examines the space between freelance and in-house work, streetwear and fashion, and transformation and counterfeiting. Graphic design remains the capability connecting every version of Mike’s work.

  5. 00:02:21

    Garage transfers created the first physical loop

    In middle school, Mike printed images onto transfer paper and ironed them onto shirts at home. Selling those garments from his car eventually produced the school fight and suspension that made the activity socially real.

  6. 00:03:19

    A laser cutter became transferable leverage

    At Goodwood, Mike learned to operate a laser for wooden jewelry. Nike later hired him to apply the same underlying skill to fabric customization, even though he had never used that exact production setup.

  7. 00:03:47

    The Nike space encouraged aggressive experimentation

    A direct-to-garment printer, vinyl cutter, laser, and a locker of blanks let Mike make dozens of custom pieces on his first day. The job became a three-year education after he left college.

  8. 00:04:17

    Creative industries still teach by doing

    The narrator places Mike inside a longer streetwear tradition of self-taught and workplace learning. Many professional skills used every week are acquired through live projects rather than classrooms.

  9. 00:04:47

    Yesterday’s outsiders have become institutions

    MAEKAN observes that the contrarians who created street culture now often occupy the establishment. Mike represents a younger version of the same run-and-test creative nucleus operating inside a mainstream field.

  10. 00:05:16

    Celebrity customization trained real-time translation

    At Nike, people such as Amar’e Stoudemire and Kanye West described elaborate jacket ideas while Mike mocked them up in minutes. Designing in front of the customer developed his fast-turn visual style.

  11. 00:05:45

    Speed became a professional identity

    The pressure to show an answer immediately means Mike thinks in days rather than multi-week design cycles. The narrator then reconnects with him through Atlanta retailer Wish and the early ICNY brand.

  12. 00:06:15

    ICNY joined personal use with a visible material system

    The first brand focused on reflective graphics for nighttime cycling. It combined Mike’s own movement through New York with screen printing, patterns, and functional apparel.

  13. 00:06:43

    Repeated crashes created the problem statement

    Mike lists collisions, dooring incidents, a damaged hand, and surgery. The accumulation made visibility more than a marketing concept; it was a recurring threat in his actual commute.

  14. 00:07:11

    Reflective clothing began as a utilitarian answer

    The garments first served Mike himself, so product decisions could be evaluated against a real activity. That personal need gave the brand an authentic center before retail expansion.

  15. 00:07:40

    The first naming mistake appeared immediately

    Mike had sold shirts before but had never organized a full brand. Ice Cold New York quickly encountered a trademark conflict with an existing business, forcing the shortened ICNY name and an improvised new explanation.

  16. 00:08:08

    Even a naming setback was converted into identity

    The acronym became ‘icy New York’ and supported the visibility idea. Mike tried to acquire the original rights, but the refusal reinforced a pattern in which every apparent step forward introduced another operating problem.

  17. 00:08:38

    The first products modified existing garments

    In 2011, Mike added reflective dots to Uniqlo and Nike socks, shirts, hats, pants, and jackets. Dots preserved visibility while reducing the weight of a full reflective sheet.

  18. 00:09:06

    Hand customization became a global wholesale brand

    A self-made wardrobe grew into an invested company with roughly three hundred retailers across more than ten countries. Hype and distribution expanded faster than Mike’s business knowledge.

  19. 00:10:03

    A freelancer became a young CEO without preparation

    At twenty-two, Mike moved from finite design assignments into responsibility for a continuing organization. The difference between completing a project and operating a company was not yet clear to him.

  20. 00:10:31

    Freelance work helped define early Kith

    Before ICNY, Mike worked closely with Ronnie Fieg on logos, marks, footwear, and apparel. Some apparently iconic identity work began as informal Illustrator exploration over meals, not a grand strategic process.

  21. 00:11:01

    Simple design can acquire a mythology after success

    The Kith box logo is remembered as highly considered because the company became important. Mike uses it to show that an effective mark can emerge quickly and that complexity is not proof of creative value.

  22. 00:11:29

    The film asks whether Mike prefers starting to sustaining

    MAEKAN contrasts a freelancer’s bounded burst with the marathon of a brand. Mike’s speed and appetite for new projects may help create identities while making long-term stewardship more difficult.

  23. 00:12:27

    Scaling can dilute the original reason to care

    Mike says repeated expansion turned a pure initial idea into many products and weakened his attachment. Once ICNY had a recognizable heart, he did not know how to guide it into adulthood.

  24. 00:13:25

    Revenue did not solve product direction

    The company approached a million dollars in sales but could not keep innovating around its promise. Commercial proof exposed rather than removed the question of what ICNY should make next.

  25. 00:13:53

    Streetwear instincts did not fully serve technical runners

    An all-over rain graphic might be compelling inside streetwear but irrelevant to someone needing dependable running performance. The aesthetic system and the functional customer were no longer aligned.

  26. 00:14:24

    Investor decisions ended Mike’s role

    In 2015, a series of control decisions forced Mike to step down and separate from ICNY. He calls the process a hostile takeover and begins explaining how the original ownership balance had shifted.

  27. 00:14:53

    Control was transferred in exchange for continued investment

    An investor requested majority ownership as protection for additional money. A family-friend lawyer advised Mike through documents while also interacting with the company and investor, creating unclear loyalties.

  28. 00:15:22

    Losing independent counsel widened the information gap

    When the lawyer became ill and withdrew, the investor obtained separate representation. Mike no longer understood the conversations or decisions occurring without him and assumed his founder status would protect him.

  29. 00:15:51

    Founding is not the same as controlling

    The narrator emphasizes that creating the intellectual property and doing years of work do not guarantee decision power. Ownership documents, bank access, and governance determine who can act when relationships collapse.

  30. 00:16:20

    Mike accepted help without preserving authority

    He began with majority ownership, then agreed to give the investor control because he valued any opportunity to grow the idea. The free-spirited decision left the investor controlling money, legal relationships, and operations.

  31. 00:17:17

    First businesses are built through costly trial

    MAEKAN connects Mike’s experience to its own founders’ early surprises. Advice helps, but questions of equity, control, and fairness still have to be learned through decisions whose consequences cannot be simulated.

  32. 00:18:17

    The creative-only role was itself a management failure

    Mike originally wanted to design while other people handled the business. Looking back, he sees that trusting the rest of the company without learning it meant surrendering the ability to protect or direct the brand.

  33. 00:18:50

    ICNY functioned as an expensive college

    He describes himself as foolish but also treats the repeated setbacks as education. Emotional reactions in meetings and resistance to opposing views made already difficult business relationships worse.

  34. 00:19:47

    A consultant questioned Mike while he promoted the brand abroad

    During an Asia customization tour, a consultant worked with the investor in New York and argued that Mike was unfit. After Mike returned, access was removed and the liquidation path became visible.

  35. 00:20:17

    The collapse resists a simple hero-and-villain account

    The film asks who was right in a system built from years of unclear roles. Password loss and exclusion were real, but the brand also faced strategic problems Mike did not yet know how to solve.

  36. 00:20:46

    Responsibility arrived after years of being told only to design

    Mike accepts that he was not prepared to steer the whole business. The contradiction is that partners had kept him away from operations, then expected him to repair those operations when they failed.

  37. 00:21:14

    Scale exceeded the founder’s prior experience

    Within a few years, a teenager who had sold shirts was responsible for full collections and major domestic and international retailers. Mike was learning honestly, but the company had no margin for that inexperience.

  38. 00:21:42

    Maturity means owning part of the outcome

    More than a year later, Mike remains unsure about every gray area but can name his contribution. The film asks whether defensiveness has given way to responsibility.

  39. 00:22:10

    Poor feedback tolerance damaged decisions

    Mike says he treated criticism of designs as personal attack, displayed anger too readily, and failed to look for the useful information inside disagreement. Those habits became explicit lessons for future partnerships.

  40. 00:22:38

    Pressure produces both discoveries and waste

    He still values intense deadlines because they force many attempts and occasionally reveal a strong idea. The same method also produces obvious failures, so speed has to coexist with honest selection.
