Full interview, in order

  1. 00:00:02

    A large installation becomes a community opportunity

    Mike says it is a dream to see the structure and understand how it was made. Taking it over matters because the project can serve ordinary community participants rather than limiting attention to celebrities and influencers.

  2. 00:00:35

    DIY is built into the Puma product

    The collaboration invites the wearer to change and assemble elements of the shoe. Mike credits Puma with providing enough direction to make the partnership workable while still allowing a small brand to experiment and push the expected narrative.

  3. 00:01:03

    The brand grew from the need to keep creating

    After losing ICNY, Mike was designing for other companies, making shirts every day, and searching for what came next. Chinatown Market became the vehicle that let him continue turning new ideas into physical clothes.

  4. 00:01:32

    Business and participation belong together

    Mike acknowledges that every project has financial goals, but says the work should also give the surrounding community a real experience. The Hong Kong activation succeeds for him because people can play, interact, and inhabit the project rather than merely view a campaign.
