Full interview, in order

  1. 00:00:00

    The print begins with a found texture

    Mike and Alex photograph concrete outside, bring the image into Photoshop, raise it to print resolution, and convert it to grayscale. An ordinary surface becomes the raw material for a one-color shirt.

  2. 00:00:29

    Halftoning converts tone into printable marks

    They increase contrast, then choose a halftone treatment. The image may look similar at normal size, but its continuous shades have been translated into dots, lines, or another discrete shape a screen can reproduce.

  3. 00:00:58

    The artwork becomes a transparency

    A logo is layered over the texture and the combined file is printed onto the coated side of transparent inkjet film. This positive will determine which parts of the light-sensitive screen remain open.

  4. 00:01:27

    The screen receives photo-sensitive emulsion

    A scoop coater spreads a generous, even layer of emulsion across the mesh. The screen must then dry in darkness so stray light does not harden the coating before exposure.

  5. 00:01:56

    Exposure depends on tight contact

    The transparency is positioned where the image should sit, glass or vacuum pressure holds it flush against the screen, and the exposure unit applies light for a measured interval. The speakers note that sunlight and a sheet of glass can substitute, but require trial and error.

  6. 00:02:25

    Washing reveals the stencil

    After exposure, water removes the unhardened image area. The screen is dried, then every unintended opening is blocked so ink can pass only through the designed stencil.

  7. 00:02:55

    Alignment and ink choice change the result

    Marks on the platen help center the screen and garment. They choose water-based ink for a softer finish, while explaining that plastisol would need heat curing in an oven.

  8. 00:03:23

    Flood, pull, and inspect

    The shirt is centered with equal sleeve spacing, ink is flooded across the screen, and a single controlled pull transfers the image. Lifting the screen reveals the completed print.

  9. 00:03:53

    The lesson is accessible production

    The workshop closes by emphasizing that a commercial shop is not required for a simple one-color hit. Careful preparation and basic equipment are enough to make a first useful sample.
