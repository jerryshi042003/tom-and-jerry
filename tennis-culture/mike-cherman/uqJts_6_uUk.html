Full interview, in order

  1. 00:00:12

    A custom request became a brand experiment

    Chinatown Market began when a friend asked Mike to design a shirt. Continued making gave the project a shape, and the Have a Nice Day graphic became an early repeatable identity.

  2. 00:00:40

    Canal Street supplied lived reference

    Mike connects the work to childhood trips with his father and years spent in New York. The brand’s early visual language came from observing Canal Street rather than adopting the reference only after it became fashionable.

  3. 00:01:08

    Ideas replace a traditional seasonal calendar

    The company releases products quickly, then treats good and bad response as information for the next experiment. Waiting for a conventional collection cycle would slow the learning process.

  4. 00:01:37

    Internet response becomes product input

    Mike describes using online data and conversation to improve physical goods. The same approach makes travel useful: Bangkok’s close creative community and vintage markets reveal how a local scene interprets global style.

  5. 00:02:06

    Climate does not erase local style

    He is struck by people maintaining distinct fashion in Bangkok’s heat. The city counters his assumption that hot weather inevitably produces generic dress and gives him another way to think about place-specific expression.
