Full interview, in order

  1. 00:00:00

    The interview begins during a period of forced distance

    The hosts welcome Mike for a remote conversation about Chinatown Market, his earlier companies, and the realities behind a seemingly fast rise. The tone is informal, but the discussion repeatedly returns to work, failure, and responsibility.

  2. 00:04:00

    A change of city altered the available references

    Mike remembers skating as marginal at his New York school and ordinary after moving to San Diego. The larger peer culture made experimentation with clothing and graphics feel like a plausible daily activity.

  3. 00:04:56

    He learned by tracing other people's processes

    Mike followed designers, identified their printers, studied software, and made shirts to sell from his car. Emulation functioned as technical research until he could understand the decisions behind the finished product.

  4. 00:08:40

    A desperate job pitch became public performance

    He created an elaborate poster campaign along Jeff Staple's route with a phone number claiming he already worked there. The stunt brought blog attention from established streetwear figures but no simple shortcut into employment.

  5. 00:12:00

    Blogs served as the first measurable feedback loop

    Mentions on HYPEBEAST, The Hundreds, and forum pages showed that a local action had traveled. Mike watched those signals closely because small coverage could connect a maker to stores and people beyond his immediate scene.

  6. 00:12:28

    Rejection redirected the energy into self-employment

    The failed attempt to be hired did not remove Mike's interest in the field. It made clear that he might need to construct the work environment and product platform he had been asking someone else to provide.

  7. 00:14:55

    ICNY concentrated design around visibility and safety

    Reflective materials gave the earlier brand a specific functional proposition and connected Mike's graphic instincts with cycling and night movement. Building it taught production and wholesale but also narrowed the category around one defining idea.

  8. 00:17:12

    Loss created an immediate need to make again

    When ICNY ended, Mike had no brand and little certainty about the next step. A friend's crude shirt request generated one design session, and that act of responding became more useful than waiting for a complete new business plan.

  9. 00:20:02

    A harmful release required more than a clever defense

    The hosts examine a public mistake and the company's apology. Mike acknowledges that speed and intent cannot erase impact; accountability means listening, correcting the action, and resisting the urge to turn the response into another marketing event.

  10. 00:23:19

    Junior should describe a role, not a permanent identity

    Mike wants early employees to gain enough experience to lead projects and eventually build independent work. Hierarchy can coordinate a team, but leaders should not use it to keep developing people psychologically small.

  11. 00:28:10

    Experimental projects need room before they look coherent

    Some ideas occupy a no-man's-land where collaborators cannot yet see the intended result. Leadership involves deciding when ambiguity signals a bad project and when the team needs protection from premature certainty.

  12. 00:31:15

    The brand grew through fast, repeated public tests

    Small runs, direct social feedback, and flexible in-house tools let MARKET try many variations without betting the company on a seasonal collection. Repetition created both audience memory and operational discipline.

  13. 00:34:24

    The Puma partnership began before MARKET had leverage

    Early conversation and mutual interest mattered because the relationship was not initiated only after the new brand became useful to a global company. Later product could build on trust and knowledge accumulated during the uncertain stage.

  14. 00:37:40

    Large collaborations still contain tedious revision

    Licensors and global partners require approvals, production constraints, and repeated changes. Mike treats that routine as the price of access rather than evidence that creative work has been replaced by administration.

  15. 00:43:58

    Education should follow a defined outcome

    Asked what young people should do, Mike questions taking on major debt by default when a student cannot name what the program unlocks. His alternative is serious self-directed work, useful entry roles, and focused learning near the desired field—not effortless independence.

  16. 00:49:00

    Partners require aligned expectations before excitement

    The closing business advice distinguishes needed complementary capability from a partner chosen merely to reduce fear. Roles, ownership, money, and decision authority have to be explicit because enthusiasm will not resolve those conflicts later.

  17. 00:51:40

    The origin story ends as an operating warning

    Mike's path validates uncredentialed experimentation, but it also records inventory losses, rejected pitches, public errors, and partnership damage. The useful lesson is to move early while making each failure improve the next system.
