What this is.

find tennis players who matter deeply in their country but do not have the distribution or backing their stories deserve.

go to the courts and places that made them. film the player, the court, the people around it, and the culture outside the fence.

make the film, photographs, writing, and physical artifact with them. return the work to the player and give the story somewhere to travel.

Jerry and Tom are not trying to own a clothing company. A shirt can open the door and prove care. The work is field production and distribution for people tennis media does not see clearly enough.

The difference.

Tom Guilmard’s Van Am is the useful reference: he drove Europe in a small van, played anyone who accepted, and documented his own 87-day journey.

His repeatable unit is Tom playing a stranger. Jerry and Tom’s repeatable unit is a locally important player, the courts and culture that produced them, and useful distribution after the crew leaves.

Asia is the first route, not the whole distinction. The distinction is that the player and place are the subject. Jerry and Tom do the access, research, filming, editing, web, physical distribution, and follow-through.

Not a vacation.

  • every stop begins with a player, host, court, or local question, not a sightseeing list
  • research and permission before arrival; call sheet, interview, match, court history, and daily backups on the ground
  • pay or cover the local people doing production work; credit every collaborator and let the player approve how they are represented
  • publish a finished player film and court record, then give the player the photographs, footage, links, and an artifact they can use
  • clothing is optional. it is evidence of involvement, not the end business

Where tennis is actually growing: players, not spectators. checked 19 August 2026.

  • China: most courts being built. 53,805 courts by August 2024, about 4,000 added since 2021, 25M+ players, up 30% in three years, plus a state plan to build the whole ecosystem. the Zheng Qinwen effect.
  • Italy: most enthusiastic per person. 6.2M practitioners in a country of 59M, federation membership up 200% since 2020, and in 2025 the tennis federation out-earned Italian football. the Sinner effect.
  • United States: biggest playing base. 27.3M players in 2025, six straight years of growth, up 54% since 2019.
  • Philippines: smallest supply meeting the newest surge. fewer than 1,000 courts for 110M people while the Eala boom lands: the same playbook as Zheng and Sinner, one league earlier. that gap is why a giveaway on a real court there travels.

Getting there.

  • LAX → MNL: Philippine Airlines nonstop, about 15h40, 17× weekly. recent round trips roughly $680–1,160 (checked 19 August 2026; live fares). one-stops via Taipei, Tokyo, or Seoul usually cheaper.
  • window: November to early December. typhoons taper by November; from mid-December seats and fares spike as overseas Filipinos fly home. book before that wave; Monday and Tuesday departures price lowest.
  • MNL → CEB: about 1h15 on Cebu Pacific or PAL, cheap if booked ahead.
  • getting around: no Uber in the Philippines. Grab is the app for cars; Angkas or JoyRide for motorbike taxis when traffic locks. Manila traffic is real: cluster Malate and Paco one day, the Quezon City and Marikina courts another. Globe or Smart eSIM from landing. stay around Makati or BGC: central to both court days.

David Choe: repeated questions / operating rules.

  • Abnormal result. You say you do not want a normal life. Are you taking the corresponding risk? If you don’t want to live like everyone else, then you literally can’t live like anyone else. Choe’s example is accepting Facebook shares while everyone else was on MySpace, not merely calling the desired outcome extraordinary. Ranch II · 01:12:14–01:13:13.
  • Permission. Are you waiting for someone to invent the job and select you? Choe says he made the Jay‑Z cover before he received the commission: Make the dream job for yourself. Ranch I · 01:10:47–01:14:28.
  • Best work. Are you sending the convenient leftover or the thing you most want to be known for? His rule for an unknown artist is: Don’t send them a shitty painting. Send them your best painting. Ranch III · 00:44:00–00:45:13.
  • Stop. Can you recognize the good moment and leave before appetite turns it into loss? How can I feel good … and then stop? Rich Roll · 01:04:14–01:06:36.

David Choe: desperation / leverage.

  • Desperation made every offer feel necessary. Choe says he accepted bad $200 illustration jobs because he needed money and the client could hear it. Gambling income temporarily removed that need; he could reject the same job or name a $2,000 price. The useful mechanism is runway and detachment, not gambling. Ranch III · 00:39:55–00:42:37.
  • Manufacture the posture without the vice. His practical substitute is to evaluate the deal as if managing another artist: eat first, remove panic, decide what the work is worth, and accept that the answer may be no. Conduct your business as if you have nothing to lose. Ranch III · 00:39:33–00:42:54.

David Choe: bet on the person / Sean Parker.

  • The choice hurt. Choe says roughly $60,000 would have been life-changing then. Facebook looked minor beside MySpace, and artist friends mocked the shares. This was abnormal behavior with a real downside, not free upside discovered later. Ranch II · 01:11:10–01:13:13.

David Choe: give the best work / earn attention.

  • Give the blue stuff. When people do not yet know why they should pay, Choe says a weak sample proves nothing. Give the best work to the hero, mentor, or person it was truly made for. The gift is complete; it is not a trade for a post, meeting, or favor. Ranch III · 00:43:50–00:45:13.
  • Bound it. He proposes one deliberate year, possibly two, of free work while keeping the day job, then charging once demand exists. This is not “work free forever,” and it does not erase the separate lesson to retain rights and hold the stated price. Ranch III · 00:45:13–00:50:09.
